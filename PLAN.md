## Problem Statement

There is no canonical educational artifact aimed at platform / infra / SRE
engineers about agent identity and authorization. AI security writing in 2026 is
dominated by prompt-injection demos and red-team tooling aimed at researchers
(PyRIT, Garak, HackAPrompt) and at AI app developers consuming agent tutorials
(awesome-llm-apps). Meanwhile, vendors — Cloudflare Agent Tokens, AWS Verified
Permissions for agents, Auth0 FGA, Permit.io, Cerbos, WorkOS, SPIFFE-derived
patterns — are quietly shipping agent-IAM features. No one has woven this into
a single coherent map a platform engineer can read in an afternoon and act on
tomorrow.

The user has:
- Deep IAM background (the unfair advantage)
- 10 hours/week, indefinitely
- Long-term horizon (multi-year), not 90-day sprint
- Joint optimization: career capital + open source community + having fun

The opportunity: become the field guide author for an emerging discipline before
the niche is taken.

## What Makes This Cool

- A book-shaped artifact ages better than 12 tweets. One canonical thing > 12
  disparate things.
- The user's IAM background is rare in this niche. Most AI security writers
  don't know workload identity / SPIFFE / least-privilege from the inside.
- The confused-deputy demo (Chapter 1) is screenshottable, viscerally scary, and
  links a classic IAM concept to the agent era. That hook compounds.
- "I wrote the field manual on agent IAM" is the kind of single-sentence
  positioning that opens conference CFPs, podcast invites, CISO DMs, and job
  offers — same artifact, four uses.

## Constraints

- 10 hours/week, indefinitely; burnout is the explicit failure mode to avoid
- Long-term horizon (multi-year). **The 90 days described below is Phase 1 of
  a multi-year arc**, not a sprint. Missing a week-12 success bar is not
  failure — the failure mode is stopping.
- Solo, one machine
- Career capital + open source community + fun are joint targets. The "fun"
  metric is measured by a single weekly gut-check: *am I still excited to
  write the next chapter?* If the answer is no for 2 weeks in a row, pivot
  (see Kill Criteria).
- X + LinkedIn dual channel from week 1; X primary for cadence, LinkedIn for
  long-form chapter launches
- Time budget audit (10 hrs/wk): ~4 hrs daily X engagement (1 tweet + 3–5
  replies × 7 days), ~5 hrs chapter work, ~1 hr LinkedIn + maintenance.
  Chapter weeks with PoC work or vendor research will steal from the X budget,
  not the other way around. **Writing comes first; engagement absorbs the
  variance.**

## Premises

1. **Audience = platform / infra / SRE engineers deploying LLM agents into
   production.** Not awesome-llm-apps devs, not security researchers, not CISOs.
   The IAM moat lives here.
2. **User is curator + explainer + IAM domain expert, not novel-research
   producer.** Synthesis + landscape mapping + canonical demo is the deliverable
   type. Shubham's "produce 100 PoCs" mechanism does not transfer.
3. **Multi-channel from day 1.** X for daily presence + chapter-launch threads;
   LinkedIn for long-form chapter rewrites. Newsletter deferred to month 2.
4. **The plan is sustainable indefinitely.** Cadence is calibrated against the
   10 hrs/week ceiling, not against ambition. Outline-driven cadence eliminates
   weekly content-decision fatigue — the actual cause of most creator burnout.
5. **Realistic week-12 floor is "platform eng send my essay to their team."**
   Anything in the Success Criteria week-4/8/12 lists is **stretch**, not
   required. Floor is real protection against the long arc; stretch is what
   makes the work worth doing. Hitting zero stretches at week 12 with the
   floor met is success, not failure.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: The Lighthouse
1 demo + 1 essay + landscape page + steady drip. Sustainable, low risk.
Differentiation thin — single demo + essay miss means the year is quiet.

### Approach B: The Drumbeat
Weekly cadence, ~12 PoCs in 90 days, Shubham-style. Highest follower growth
ceiling, but burnout cliff at week 6–8 is well-documented. Rejected on the
no-burnout constraint.

### Approach C: The Field Manual (CHOSEN)
One coherent book-shaped artifact, 6–8 chapters. The confused-deputy demo
is the worked example in Chapter 1. Vendor landscape is Chapter 3. Each chapter
ships as essay + LinkedIn long-form + X thread + GitHub commit.

## Recommended Approach

**Working title: "Agent Identity — A Field Manual for Platform Engineers"**

Single coherent body of work, released chapter-by-chapter, with X+LinkedIn
chapter launches and a daily X presence between chapters. One decision (the
outline) drives the entire 90 days, eliminating weekly content-decision fatigue.

### Outline (week 1 deliverable — this is the critical path)

**Part I — Why Agent Identity**
- Ch 1: The Confused Deputy Comes for Agents *(with PoC demo)*
- Ch 2: Why Prompt-Injection Defenses Aren't Enough *(the lethal-trifecta gap
  that IAM fills; Meta's Agents Rule of Two as context)*

**Part II — The Landscape**
- Ch 3: Who's Solving What *(Cloudflare Agent Tokens, AWS Verified Permissions,
  Auth0 FGA, Permit.io, Cerbos, WorkOS, OWASP Agentic Top 10 mapping — what

**Part III — Patterns**
- Ch 5: Least Privilege for Agents *(the OG IAM pattern re-applied)*
- Ch 6: Identity-Aware Memory & Retrieval *(multi-tenant agent isolation,

- Ch 7: Auditability & the Agent as a Principal

**Part IV — Future**
- Ch 8: What Agent IAM Looks Like in 2028

  week 1 verifying deadlines. "Submit a CFP" without a named target is theatre.

- **B5 — Tier-1 reply list.** Pin the exact 20 X/LinkedIn accounts you will

  Schulhoff, Christian Posta, founders/eng leads at Permit.io, Cerbos, Auth0,

**OPTIONAL** (resolve in flight):

## Kill Criteria (when to pivot)

The plan dies if:

- **Week 4:** Ch 1 not shipped AND the slip has no specific named blocker. (A
  named blocker — "PoC won't work" — is a fixable thing. A vague "I'm behind"
  is the founder-killing pattern.)
- **Week 6:** "Am I still excited?" gut-check is "no" for 2 weeks in a row.
- **Week 8:** Two chapters shipped, but zero engagement of any kind (no replies,
  no link-backs, no DMs from anyone in the target audience). This is signal
  the audience is wrong, not the cadence.

The pivot in each case is *not* "give up on AI security." The pivot is to
re-run /office-hours with the new data and design Phase 2 accordingly.

### Critical-path issues (P1 — these will bite)

**1. Day-4 evening is overloaded.** The plan stacks: integrate reviewer
feedback (B1) + lock outline + resolve B2 + resolve B3 + create public repo
+ push README on a single evening. Any one slipping (especially B1, which
depends on external humans) cascades into the day-5 tweet. Decouple:
- Move B2 (PoC framework) and B3 (name) to **day 2**, before the reviewer
  send. They're ≤1 hour each and don't need outline feedback.
- Day 4 evening becomes only: integrate B1 feedback → lock outline →
  push repo → make public. Single decision per step.

**2. B1 has no fallback when a reviewer ghosts.** Plan says pick 2-3
reviewers, give them 48 hours, integrate their feedback by day 4 evening.
Real-life base rate for "senior person responds to a content-review ask in
48h": ~50%. Mitigation:
- Pick **4 reviewers, send to 3**, accept 2 responses as success. Add a
  filter: "from people who'd respond to me personally within a day"
  (cold asks won't make the window).
- If neither responds by day 4 noon: lock the outline without external
  input. The forcing function (day-5 tweet) wins over the validation.

**3. Day-5 tweet promises something the plan's contingency contradicts.**
Plan's draft: "Outline here, Ch 1 in 2 weeks: [link]." The scope-cut rule
allows shipping Ch 1 as "theory + diagram" if PoC isn't working. That's
fine internally, but the tweet's implicit promise to a platform-eng
audience is the working PoC (the confused-deputy demo is the whole hook).
Either:
- Tighten the tweet: "Outline here. Ch 1 essay in 2 weeks; PoC follow-up:"
  — pre-frames the contingency, no surprise.
- Or wait until day 7 to tweet, after day-6 PoC smoke test proves
  feasibility. Costs 2 days of public commitment, buys honest framing.

### High-leverage gaps (P2 — worth fixing in Week 1)

**4. Outline is the most consequential artifact of the entire 90 days
but Week 1 budgets ~5 hours of chapter work for it.** The plan even says
"the most important artifact of the whole plan." Recommend:
- Reallocate the entire ~5 hrs/wk "chapter work" budget into the outline
  for week 1 (week 1 has no chapter to write).
- Treat draft-1 as throwaway. Write it in 2 hours, then a second draft
  in 2 hours, then send draft-2 (not draft-1) for review.

**5. B5 (reply list) deferred to weekend kills the daily-X muscle for
most of week 1.** Plan says "X + LinkedIn dual channel from week 1" with
~4 hrs/wk on engagement. If the 20-account list isn't pinned until day 6-7,
days 1-5 have no engagement target, no muscle memory. Move B5 to day 2
(it's a 30-min task — write 20 handles into a file).

**6. CFP picks without abstract drafts.** B4 is "pick 2 conferences."
Good. But Week 12 then has to "write CFP abstract from scratch + submit"
inside the lighter pattern-recap week — that is too much. Add to Week 1:
draft one ~100-word abstract per chosen CFP. Half an hour each. Week 12
becomes "polish + submit," which fits the slack.

**7. Repo scaffold should include placeholder chapter README.md files,
not just the top-level outline.** Plan says "Push the outline as
README.md." Click-through experience for a curious platform engineer
visiting the public repo is "1 README." Strong version: 8 chapter
directories, each with a 1-paragraph chapter.md stub matching the outline.
"This thing is real and structured" lands very differently. Costs
20 minutes on day 4 evening. Big perception delta.
